## Already covered and newly useful

The ratified *Genre Craft and Success Measures* already treats categories as overlapping, gives room to omission and unresolved meaning, and makes review conditional on a work's intended experience. Page 19 explicitly distinguishes ambiguity in fiction from clarity in instructions and controls. That distinction is not a discovery here. Related material already separates character belief from world truth and fictional metaphysics from claims about the real world.

The inspected approved source-design baseline contains the label **magical realism**. A label does not establish a developed treatment or prove what a current live system does. No dedicated discussion was located in the inspected 25-page genre reference or the 55-page *Cross-Genre Research Reader v1*. Whether a dedicated optional profile exists elsewhere remains unresolved; absence has not been established.

Three additions are worth retaining as research candidates:

- **Separate presentation decisions.** A definite occurrence need not receive a causal explanation. Characters can respond differently without the narrator resolving their disagreement.
- **Keep visual and literary routes distinct.** An ordinary scene made newly strange through its depiction is not the same creative decision as an impossible event stated as fact within a story.
- **Situate interpretation.** Ask which particular people, histories and institutions shape an account, rather than dividing whole cultures into rational and magical camps.

These are refinements to existing coverage, not replacements. The aim is to give an author more deliberate choices without making Blackwish's creative space smaller.

## What the sources support

**Wikipedia as a research map.** The article presents overlapping histories and disputed definitions, not a settled checklist. Its useful leads include reticence, material mystery, competing worldviews and historical critique. Its visual-art discussion does not require an overt supernatural addition. Broad cultural contrasts and comparisons with fantasy need scrutiny rather than conversion into defaults. [Wikipedia on magical realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magical_realism).

**Visual history.** Tate connects Franz Roh's 1925 usage with a painterly shift toward sharply rendered, unsettling imagery. This supports distinguishing a visual procedure from later literary arguments; it does not establish a recipe for prose. [Tate catalogue description](https://shop.tate.org.uk/magic-realism-art-in-weimar-germany-1919-33/21160.html).

**Literary history and reticence.** Warnes treats early Carpentier's account as historically situated and concerned with affirming particular worldviews. He also describes Chanady's model as maintaining natural and supernatural codes without explaining one away. Reticence, in that account, protects their coexistence. Warnes's broader distinction between affirmation and irreverence cautions against assuming that every work has the same attitude toward belief. [Warnes on magical realism, especially pages 5 to 9](https://www.blackwellpublishing.com/pdf/compass/lico_106.pdf).

**A terminology caution.** Chanady explicitly distinguishes the fantastic from fantasy literature in general and discusses modes that can appear across genres. Her opening comparison distinguishes an accepted marvelous setting from an apparent supernatural intrusion that threatens an everyday order. This is not a license to treat all fantasy as magical realism's opposite. [Chanady publisher preview, introduction and pages 1 to 5](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781000627657_A38537740/preview-9781000627657_A38537740.pdf).

**A different critical emphasis.** Slemon emphasizes continuing tension between narrative codes without either gaining supremacy. In readings of two Canadian novels, he connects compressed temporal scales and competing histories with postcolonial discourse. These are situated arguments, not requirements that every work preserve ambiguity, use cyclic time or express one politics. His account and Chanady's account should not be silently merged into a single uncontested definition. [Slemon, printed pages 10 to 11, 14 to 16 and 20 to 21](https://ojs.library.ubc.ca/index.php/canlit/article/download/194263/190168).

**A warning about classification.** Connell's indexed opening criticizes classifications that reserve modernity for Western writing while assigning magic and alleged primitiveness to non-Western writing. The criticism concerns how literature is categorized; it does not establish a population's shared worldview. Only the indexed opening was accessible, not the full article. [Connell on definitions of magic realism](https://journalhosting.ucalgary.ca/index.php/ariel/article/view/34011/28050).

## Original applications for Blackwish

The following are editorial proposals arising from the comparison, not additional source definitions or validated measures of literary quality.

### Separate what happens from what it means

An author can make a small working note about a scene: what happens, what the narrator says about it, what the characters do, and what explanation the telling promises. Those questions need not have matching answers. A story could establish that a clock returns lost minutes, let two siblings use them differently, and never explain the clock. Another story could make the apparent anomaly a clue. A third could explain a reproducible power. These are different commitments, not a quality ranking.

This distinction is useful when an automatic impulse to explain would alter the intended work. Before proposing a mechanism, ask whether the author wants one. Equally, an explicit mechanism is not an artistic failure: it may be exactly what another story needs. An unanswered question is not inherently richer than an answered one.

The boundary is continuity. If a scene states an event as fact, a later passage should not casually treat it as merely someone's guess. A narrator may be deliberately unreliable or a story may revise earlier understanding, but the revision needs to belong to the chosen telling. Interpretive openness does not make every contradiction purposeful.

### Let ordinary responsibilities remain visible

A marvel need not suspend the rest of a character's life. A person may still have a parcel to deliver, a plant to water or a promise to keep. For Blackwish, the practical question is whether the extraordinary occurrence changes an action, relationship, perception or obligation that matters in this particular scene.

That is an invitation, not a requirement that every image advance a plot. A beautiful or puzzling detail can be worth keeping for its rhythm or presence. The useful counterexample is an author who intentionally wants a still, observational passage: demanding an obstacle, lesson or payoff would damage it. The author can explain the intended effect without proving that every reader will experience it.

### Try a visual version without adding a new power

An optional comparison draft could keep every object ordinary and change only attention: the distance between two chairs, a row of identical cups, a door left just short of its frame, or the silence around a routine action. Another draft could introduce an impossible object. Comparing them may reveal whether the desired effect depends on an event or on a way of looking.

Neither draft earns a genre label merely by existing. This exercise is especially useful before an adaptation substitutes spectacle for a quieter passage. A soundtrack, camera angle or caption can change what a scene seems to assert. A sinister music cue might turn an everyday encounter into a threat; an explanatory caption might close an interpretation the prose left open. These are possible changes to notice, not bans on music or explanation.

### Give disagreements particular owners

An original scene can let members of one household disagree without making either person stand for an entire culture. A character might trust a family recollection in one matter and an official record in another. The author can ask why this person gives this account authority here, and what practical choice follows.

For real traditions, names or histories, the necessary research becomes specific to that material. This overview cannot supply authenticity or permission to generalize. A wholly invented history should remain clearly invented rather than borrowing the appearance of documentation.

A related temporal prompt is: what does the past still require of someone now? A recurring task or contested place name can matter more than a complicated chronology. There is no need to insert generational history into every short scene. If the present action is sufficient, leave it sufficient.

## Two original G rated microexamples

These independent illustrative miniatures are not canon, generated runtime outputs, or continuations of a current IP Cascade fixture. They demonstrate choices rather than certify a genre classification.

### The repaired clock

Every Tuesday, the station clock returned the eleven minutes it had lost when the old platform closed. Iris used them to water the pots beside the ticket window. Her brother spent them checking whether the train from their childhood had appeared.

The new timetable counted the minutes as a delay. Iris wrote "watering" in the margin.

That morning her brother brought a second watering can.

**What this version tries.** The event is stated directly. The siblings differ in their response; the final action changes their relationship without explaining the clock. The timetable supplies a third, administrative account of the same occurrence.

**A useful counterversion.** Make the clock ordinary and the eleven minutes a shared habit. If the emotional movement survives, the author can compare what the impossible event adds rather than assuming it must remain. Alternatively, explain a clock mechanism and let repairing it become the story. That would pursue a different interest, not expose the original as defective.

### The neighborhood directory

The printer delivered the new neighborhood directory in three boxes. On each page, the old street names showed through the new ones, although the paper was thick.

Nell could read the road to her grandmother's house only when she held the book sideways. The delivery driver needed the current names. They carried a copy between them, turning it at each corner.

At the last house, someone had left two labels beside the bell. Nell added a third: "Please knock. Bell being repaired."

**What this version tries.** Different readings support a shared practical task. The ending leaves the directory unexplained while settling one ordinary instruction. That last distinction illustrates existing Blackwish coverage; it is not a new interface principle.

**A useful counterversion.** Resolve the print effect as a production error and make tracing the error the plot. Or retain it while changing who can read which names. Each choice changes the emphasis. The miniature deliberately specifies no real naming history; attaching it to a real community would require particular research rather than an assumed colonial backstory.

## Optional author metaprompts

Use any prompt that helps a particular draft; skip those that do not. They are conversation starters, not mandatory fields or acceptance tests.

- What must the reader know happened, and what may remain open?
- What explanation, if any, has this telling promised?
- How does each character respond, beyond accepting or rejecting the occurrence?
- Which ordinary choice still matters after it happens?
- Could exact material description serve the scene without a supernatural addition?
- Whose account receives authority, and what gives it that position here?
- Which details are researched, invented, uncertain or intentionally unspecified?
- What might an adaptation accidentally explain, sensationalize or turn into a controllable power?

A note answering one useful question may be enough. The author need not complete the list before writing.

## Three bounded reviews

These are three distinct text-only passes. POINT names the objective; BRIDGE checks support; PATH follows the implication; SHADOW challenges it. They do not certify literary success or grant adoption authority.

### Evidence and genre distinctions

**POINT** Identify the smallest supported addition to existing coverage. **BRIDGE** Keep the article's leads, the scholars' differing accounts and this brief's original applications distinguishable. **PATH** Retain the useful distinctions without turning them into a universal definition. **SHADOW** Reject the claims that every magical-realist work must be ambiguous, that all fantasy is its opposite, or that one historical strand explains every later practice.

**Stopping condition:** the retained claim has a named source or is explicitly an editorial proposal, and its limitation is visible. A disputed boundary remains disputed.

### Creative choices and coherence

**POINT** Preserve the intended scene while exploring an alternative. **BRIDGE** Compare the stated event, narrator treatment, character responses and explanation promised by the actual text. **PATH** Change one choice in a private comparison draft and describe what the change gains or loses. **SHADOW** Test whether explanation erases a wanted effect, or whether withholding only conceals information the story owes its reader.

**Stopping condition:** the alternatives and their tradeoffs are clear enough for the author to choose. No reader experiment or genre score is implied.

### Agency and situated context

**POINT** Keep people and the author from becoming servants of a label. **BRIDGE** Check who supplies an interpretation, what contextual evidence supports it and which creative decisions remain the user's. **PATH** Preserve meaningful choices, name research gaps and leave adoption for explicit manual ratification. **SHADOW** Challenge cultural sorting, automatic outsider authority, hidden interaction penalties and any conversion of this brief into mandatory rules.

**Stopping condition:** no unresolved question is being silently converted into a cultural fact, a user obligation or permission to deploy. Reopen the relevant question if material new evidence appears.
